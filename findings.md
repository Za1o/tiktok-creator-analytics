# Findings — interview summary

Project: TikTok Creator Analytics — performance analysis of a short-form entertainment account I operate and manage.
All figures verified in TikTok Studio on November 24, 2026. Titles and creator details are anonymized in the public datasets; metrics are as reported by TikTok Studio.

## The account in one line

198 posts, 370 followers, 52.5K likes — and about 92% of its traffic comes from Search, not the For You feed.

## Three findings I can explain

1. **Search drives discovery, so I optimize for search.** Traffic source: Search 91.7%, Personal profile 4.2%, For You 4.0%, Following 0.1% (same in the 7-day and 28-day views). That is why video titles and captions are built around clear, searchable keywords describing the content. My #1 video (Jul 7, 2026, shown as 1M views, 3,005 likes, 187 shares, 581 saves) got 99.1% of its traffic from Search and added +235 followers.

2. **A series launch explains the spike, in numbers.** Nov 16–22, 2026 totaled 63,938 video views (the export CSV daily rows sum exactly to the Studio total). Nov 16–18 averaged 972 views/day. Nov 19 hit 17,478 (17.98× baseline) and Nov 20 peaked at 28,041 (28.85× baseline). Nov 19–20 together were 45,519 views — 71.19% of the week — coinciding with a new multi-part storyline series launched Nov 19, led by Series A, Part 1 (13K window views, 99.2% Search).

3. **Serialized storyline content is the account's repeatable strategy.** 8 of the all-time top 10 are serialized storyline moments from the same recurring series. The one clear exception to the Search pattern in the top 10 is a gift-themed video (Jul 24, 2026; 54K views), which got 61.4% of its traffic from For You vs 32.0% from Search — a useful reminder that channel-level patterns do not describe every video.

## What I am careful about

- Windowed numbers (7-day, 28-day) and all-time numbers answer different questions — I keep them separate and never mix them.
- Posts-page view counts are rounded (1M shown; 997K in the Nov 19 audit), so close rankings are approximate.
- One spike tied to one series launch is evidence for what to test next, not a guarantee.
